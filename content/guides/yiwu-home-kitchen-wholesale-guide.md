## Why Yiwu for Home & Kitchen Wholesale

Yiwu International Trade City is the largest wholesale market of its kind in the world. For home and kitchen products specifically, Yiwu offers three structural advantages that no other sourcing destination can match:

1. **Supplier density** — Within a single market building, you can find 50+ factories making stainless steel tumblers, 30+ making ceramic tableware, and 20+ making kitchen utensils. Comparison shopping happens in hours, not weeks.
2. **Small-batch flexibility** — Yiwu factories are built for small commodity wholesale. Many accept 100-500 piece MOQs, compared to 5,000+ in Guangdong or Shenzhen manufacturing hubs.
3. **Mixed-order consolidation** — This is the killer advantage. You can buy 20 SKUs from 10 different factories, and a Yiwu trading partner consolidates everything into one container shipment.

## Planning Your Sourcing Strategy

### Step 1: Define Your Product Scope

The biggest mistake new buyers make is trying to source too many categories at once. A focused approach works better:

- **Start with 3-5 related categories** — For example, drinkware + kitchen gadgets + storage. These share similar buyer demographics and can be combined into mixed orders.
- **Select 20-50 SKUs for your first order** — This is enough to fill a small LCL shipment or test market demand without overcommitting.
- **Prioritize products with clear retail demand** — Look at Amazon Best Sellers, Walmart trending items, and your own market research. Don't source products just because they look interesting at the market.

### Step 2: Research Suppliers Before You Arrive

Before visiting Yiwu or sending inquiries:

- Browse our [product catalog](/products/) to identify specific SKUs
- Check Amazon reviews for similar products to understand quality expectations
- Prepare a spec sheet for each product (material, size, color, packaging)
- Set a target FOB price based on your retail margin calculation

### Step 3: Market Visit or Remote Sourcing

**If you visit Yiwu in person:**

- Plan 3-5 full days for market visits
- Hire a local sourcing agent or work with us for factory visits
- Collect business cards, samples, and quotation sheets from every booth
- Take photos of products, packaging, and booth numbers
- Don't commit to anything on the first day — compare before negotiating

**If you source remotely (most common):**

- Send us your SKU list and target quantities
- We collect samples from 2-3 factories per product for comparison
- We send you a consolidated sample package with quotation sheets
- You approve samples and specifications before placing the order

## Understanding MOQ in Yiwu

MOQ in Yiwu works differently from other manufacturing hubs. Here is what to expect:

| Product Type | Typical MOQ | Notes |
|-------------|-------------|-------|
| Stock items (no customization) | 50-200 pcs | Many factories keep inventory |
| Custom logo printing | 200-500 pcs | Setup cost amortized over quantity |
| Custom color (Pantone matching) | 500-1,000 pcs | Powder coating or anodizing minimums |
| Custom packaging | 500-1,000 pcs | Printing plate setup |
| Full ODM product | 3,000-5,000 pcs | New mold or significant redesign |

**Key insight**: "Mixed MOQ" is common in Yiwu. You can often combine multiple SKUs from the same factory to reach the MOQ threshold, rather than buying 500 pcs of a single SKU.

## Sample Process

Samples are the most critical step in the sourcing process. Never place a bulk order without approving a sample.

1. **Initial sample** — Request 1-2 pieces to check material, size, and quality. Cost: $3-8 per piece + shipping.
2. **Comparison sample** — If multiple factories make the same product, order samples from 2-3 suppliers. Side-by-side comparison reveals quality differences.
3. **Customized sample** — After selecting a supplier, request a sample with your logo and color. Cost: $15-50 depending on customization. Lead time: 5-10 days.
4. **Pre-production sample** — Before mass production, the factory produces one final sample for your sign-off. This is the gold standard reference for quality inspection.

## Quality Control Essentials

Quality issues are the #1 cause of sourcing disputes. Here is a practical QC framework:

**Before production:**
- Confirm material specifications in writing (e.g., "304 stainless steel, 0.4mm wall thickness")
- Approve a pre-production sample with your signature/date
- Specify acceptable tolerance ranges for dimensions

**During production:**
- Request production photos at 30%, 60%, and 90% completion
- For large orders, arrange an in-line inspection (we can do this for you)

**Before shipment:**
- Final random inspection at AQL 2.5 level
- Check: dimensions, surface finish, functionality, packaging, labeling
- Reject the batch if critical defects exceed AQL limits

This is where a Yiwu-based partner adds the most value. Here is how it works:

1. You place orders with 5-10 different factories for different products
2. Each factory ships goods to our Yiwu warehouse
3. We inspect every batch upon arrival
4. We consolidate all products into a single container
5. You receive one shipment with all your products, one set of shipping documents

**Cost savings**: Consolidation reduces per-SKU shipping cost by 30-60% compared to shipping each factory's goods separately. It also simplifies customs clearance and warehouse receiving on your end.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1. **Don't skip the sample step** — Photos and videos are not a substitute for holding the physical product.
2. **Don't negotiate on price before approving quality** — A cheaper price often means cheaper materials.
3. **Don't assume "304 stainless steel" means the same thing everywhere** — Specify thickness, finish, and certification requirements.
4. **Don't forget packaging in your cost calculation** — Custom color boxes, bubble wrap, and palletizing add cost.
5. **Don't ship without final inspection** — Once goods leave China, returns are practically impossible.
6. **Don't ignore lead time buffers** — Production delays happen. Plan for 7-10 days of buffer beyond quoted lead time.
